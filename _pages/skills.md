## Quantitative & Causal Methods

I trained in econometrics before moving into HCI, so I approach study design asking what would actually identify an effect, not just whether a test is significant. I run and interpret regression models (OLS, fixed-effects/panel, logistic) in R and Stata, and I bring that same scrutiny for confounds and robustness to human-subjects experiments, a lens that's still uncommon in HCI.

## Qualitative & Participatory Methods

I run interviews, think-aloud studies, and participatory design sessions, and combine them with the quantitative work above rather than treating them as separate tracks. Participatory design lets me bring the people affected by a system into its design, which matters directly for the human-AI collaboration questions I study.

## Design Practice

I've TA'ed a UI/UX design course, so I can prototype and critique interfaces myself, which makes it easier to work alongside designers rather than only evaluate their output after the fact.
