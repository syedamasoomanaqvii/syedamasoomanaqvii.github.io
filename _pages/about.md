I am a 2nd year PhD. student in Computer Science at the University of Minnesota where I am advised by [Lana Yarosh](https://lanayarosh.com). I am a part of the [GroupLens Research Lab](https://grouplens.org). I study how humans and AI make decisions together, with a focus on how group composition, experience, and expertise shape that collaboration. My work spans human-centered AI, interpretability, and explainable ML, where I evaluate existing tools and develop theory-driven design paradigms aligned with human cognitive and socio-organizational norms. I aim to offer  frameworks and guidance for responsible, reliable human–AI workflows.

My training spans both sides of the quant-qual divide: I came to HCI through econometrics, so I think about study design in terms of causal identification, not just correlation, and I pair that with the qualitative and participatory methods more common in HCI. I've also TA'ed user interface and interaction design, so I can prototype and critique design work directly rather than only study it from the outside. That combination is uncommon in HCI, and it shapes how I evaluate human-AI systems. I want claims about what makes collaboration work to hold up both as a statistical result and as a lived experience. See [Skills & Methods](/skills/) for more.
